# Source Taxonomy and Discovery Map

Use this taxonomy to plan coverage. It is a set of examples, not a claim that every source is accessible, current, exhaustive, or endorsed. Select sources based on country, language, industry, seniority, and the user's constraints. Add newly discovered sources only after checking their relevance and provenance.

## Source authority tiers

| Tier | Source class | Best evidence for | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Employer-controlled career page, recruitment microsite, official employer account | Role identity, official requirements, application route, employer statements | Check URL/domain and role status; an official page can still be stale |
| B | Government, intergovernmental organization, university, or institution's own recruitment portal | Public-sector/institutional vacancy terms and deadlines | Confirm the exact institution and country/site |
| C | Established job board or named recruitment firm | Discovery, listing details, recruiter context | Reconfirm on employer source where possible; listings may be duplicated or outdated |
| D | Public social post by an identifiable employer/recruiter/hiring manager | Hiring announcement or referral lead | Account authenticity and vacancy status may be uncertain |
| E | Public company news, regulatory filings, investor relations, press release | Business event or workforce statement | Business event is not proof of a job opening |
| F | Search snippets, reposts, scraped copies, anonymous discussion | Search lead only | Never treat as final verification without stronger evidence |

Authority is claim-specific: an employer's annual report is strong evidence of a reported expansion, but not of an unlisted vacancy. An official vacancy page is stronger for job existence than a third-party repost.
## Global region planning

Use these broad region families to check search coverage when the user's request is global; they are planning labels, not a promise that every country or source was searched:

- **Asia:** Indonesia, Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Japan, South Korea, China, India.
- **Europe:** Germany, Netherlands, France, UK, Switzerland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland, Belgium, Ireland.
- **North America:** US, Canada.
- **Middle East:** UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain.
- **Oceania:** Australia, New Zealand.
- **Africa:** South Africa, Kenya, Nigeria, Egypt, plus other relevant countries.
- **Global / remote.**

These are non-exhaustive planning examples, not a statement of coverage. Research and verify each selected country separately; do not treat regional/global labels as evidence that a country was searched or that a role is eligible there.

For each selected region, name the countries and sources actually covered. Treat `global` or `remote` as a search constraint to verify per posting, not as evidence of worldwide eligibility. These examples and region families are non-exhaustive; adapt coverage to the user's scope, language, work authorization, and the tools available.


## Global job discovery examples

Examples include LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, Glassdoor, Google Jobs search surfaces, Wellfound, ZipRecruiter, Monster, CareerBuilder, SimplyHired, Talent.com, Jooble, Remote OK, We Work Remotely, Himalayas, Remotive, Working Nomads, Jobspresso, FlexJobs, and Tech in Asia Jobs. Discover regionally important alternatives for the target country. Do not state that a portal was checked unless the active session actually searched or opened it.

## Indonesia-focused examples

Potential discovery routes include JobStreet Indonesia, Glints, Kalibrr, Dealls, Karir.com, KitaLulus, Loker.id, TopKarir, KarirHub, Tech in Asia Jobs, employer career sites, public BUMN/government recruitment notices, university career centers, professional associations, and public job-fair notices.

Useful Indonesian wording includes:

- `lowongan kerja`, `dibutuhkan segera`, `rekrutmen`, `kesempatan berkarier`, `bergabung bersama kami`
- `penerimaan karyawan`, `open recruitment`, `walk-in interview`, `talent pool`
- `fresh graduate`, `profesional berpengalaman`, `management trainee`, `program magang`

Terms vary by employer and may be used in old or copied posts. Validate the post's date, employer, and application route.

## Employer and institutional sources

Search employer career pages, official job portals, graduate and internship programs, management-trainee portals, talent communities, executive-search announcements, and public hiring campaigns. For international and development-sector roles, consider the United Nations system, World Bank Group, Asian Development Bank, International Finance Corporation, International Labour Organization, ASEAN bodies, public universities, research institutions, international NGOs, and publicly advertised embassy roles. Check the hiring entity's own site and eligibility rules.

## Public social and community sources

Potential public surfaces include LinkedIn, X, Facebook, Instagram, Threads, Reddit, TikTok, YouTube, and public Telegram channels. Search only publicly accessible content through permitted tools. Treat public posts as leads unless the author, employer, and role can be corroborated. Do not enter private groups, access direct messages, or infer private identities.

## Corporate-intelligence sources

Use official press releases, company announcements, investor-relations pages, annual reports, public financial disclosures, corporate presentations, acquisition/funding announcements, market-entry notices, facility openings, new business units, and product launches. Cite the original source and date. Convert the event into a **hypothesis about relevant job families**, never a vacancy claim unless a separate job advertisement exists.

## Industry source and language adaptation

For Banking, Financial Services, Accounting, Manufacturing, Energy, Oil & Gas, Technology, AI, Healthcare, Pharmaceuticals, Education, Telecom, Logistics, Supply Chain, Retail, FMCG, Construction, Mining, Hospitality, Consulting, Legal, Government, Non-profit, and Creative Industries:

1. Identify the function's actual responsibilities and local credential requirements.
2. Search relevant professional associations, trade publications, employer clusters, and specialist recruiters when public and permitted.
3. Add industry vocabulary and adjacent job families to the query plan.
4. Keep adjacent roles separate when duties, authority, or qualifications differ.

For international searches, consider local-language titles and search terms. Example German terms may include `Stellenangebot`, `Karriere`, `Finanzmanager`, and `Bewerbung`; validate grammar and title equivalence for the specific role. A translation is a discovery aid, not proof of a match.

## Source discovery procedure

For each search brief, create a source matrix with: region/country, role/industry, source category, candidate domains, actual access status, query used, and evidence level. Discover new domains from official company references, credible search results, and institutional directories. Before relying on a new domain, check who operates it, whether it is the original publisher, and what exact claim it supports.

## Coverage language

Use bounded wording such as “searched employer career pages and three job-board search surfaces accessible in this session.” Never use “all sources,” “complete global coverage,” or “every job on the internet.” If search engines or platforms could not be accessed, list them as unsearched/unavailable rather than silently omitting them.
